PokéDex Hub Master Guide

Learn how to utilize the full power of PokéDex Hub: explore complete competitive databases, build championship-caliber teams with 4-tier diagnostic verification, and master the turn-based battle simulator against self-learning AI.

🌟 Overview & Philosophy

PokéDex Hub is built from the ground up to solve the fragmentation of modern competitive Pokémon tools. Instead of having to open a Pokédex in one tab, a damage calculator in another, Smogon in a third, and Pokémon Showdown in a fourth, PokéDex Hub unifies everything into one seamless, blazingly fast web application.

⚡ Instant Offline-First Caching

All 1,025+ Pokémon, 900+ moves, abilities, items, and evolutions are persisted to local JSON disk caches. Pages load in sub-10 milliseconds without external API lag.

👥 Tournament-Grade Teambuilder

Pre-populated with 60 championship archetypes. Includes real-time offensive coverage checks against all 18 types, defensive weakness matrices, and dynamic speed tier comparisons.

🏟️ Turn-by-Turn Simulator

Play against an AI that scales from casual random plays (Level 1) to Champion-level minimax predictive lookahead (Level 100) with a self-learning knowledge cache.

💡
Pro Tip: All user data, custom teams, and favorite Pokémon persist automatically in your browser's LocalStorage and the local server cache. No login or cloud account is required.

🧠 How the Championship AI Uses the Battle Knowledge Cache

Unlike static scripted bots that treat every battle in isolation, the PokéDex Hub Championship AI features an active **Battle Knowledge Cache** (persisted in data/battle_knowledge_cache.json). This allows the AI to learn from its past wins and losses against your teams over time.

  • 1. Automatic Post-Battle Learning:

    Whenever a match ends, the AI analyzes the entire battle log. It looks at every knockout: which Pokémon fainted, which Pokémon dealt the finishing blow, and which move was used. If that knockout contributed to winning the overall match, the AI registers that move as a high-value tactic against that specific species.

  • 2. Empirical Win-Rate Weighting:

    The AI tracks a weighted win-rate for every observed matchup (e.g. kingambit_vs_metagross or dragapult_vs_baxcalibur). The win-rate updates using an Exponential Moving Average:
    $$\text{WinRate}_{\text{new}} = (\text{WinRate}_{\text{current}} \times 0.8) + (\text{Target} \times 0.2)$$
    This prevents a lucky critical hit from skewing the AI's memory while ensuring consistent tournament trends are rapidly remembered.

  • 3. Live In-Battle Move Selection (Difficulty ≥ 70):

    When you set the AI slider to Difficulty 70 or higher (Elite Four and World Champion tiers), the AI checks its Knowledge Cache at the start of every turn:

    • It compares its active Pokémon against your active Pokémon to find matching historical records.
    • If it has a record of a move that previously secured a KO and match victory, that move receives a massive score boost of up to +40.0 points ($\text{WinRate} \times 40.0$).
    • This directly influences the AI's decision to strike with proven counters or activate its Omni Ring (Mega Evolution, Gigantamax, or Terastallization) on turn 1 to replicate past winning conditions!
  • 4. Viewing and Resetting AI Knowledge:

    You can view real-time AI knowledge statistics (total battles analyzed, AI win/loss ratio, and recent tactical debriefs) on the Developer Diagnostics Hub on the Home page (/). If you want to reset the AI's memory and face a completely fresh computer opponent, simply click the red "Reset AI Knowledge" button or send a POST request to /api/simulator/knowledge/reset.

  • Move Selection: Choose from 4 move buttons displaying move type, category (Physical 💥 / Special 🔮 / Status 🛡️), Base Power, Accuracy, and PP.
  • 💍 Omni Ring System: Each trainer may activate exactly ONE generational gimmick per match from the Omni Ring dock:
    • 💎 Terastallization (TERA): Shifts defensive and offensive typing into your pure Tera type for the entire match.
    • 🧬 Mega Evolution (MEGA): Mega Evolution strictly validates that your active Pokémon is holding its canonical Mega Stone (e.g. Charizardite X for Mega X, Charizardite Y for Mega Y, Lucarionite, Mewtwonite X/Y, Rayquaza knowing Dragon Ascent, and upcoming canonical forms such as Mega Z). If the stone is held, it mega-evolves instantly into its ultimate form with enhanced base stats, ability shifts, and custom sprites.
    • ⚡ Gigantamax (G-MAX): Transforms the Pokémon into a colossal titan for 3 turns with 1.5x Max HP and converts all moves into powerful Max Moves:
      • Max Guard: All status moves (e.g. Protect, Roost, Swords Dance) become Max Guard (priority +4, protecting 100% from all attacks including Max Moves).
      • Standard Max Moves: Damaging moves convert by type into moves like Max Quake (+1 SpD), Max Strike (-1 Spe), Max Knuckle (+1 Atk), Max Flare (sets Sun), Max Geyser (sets Rain), Max Lightning (sets Electric Terrain), and Max Hailstorm (sets Snow).

  • Choice Lock Enforcement: If holding a Choice Band, Choice Specs, or Choice Scarf, after your first move is chosen, the other three move buttons are disabled and tagged LOCKED until the Pokémon switches out (suspended while Dynamaxed).
  • Assault Vest Status Protection: Holding an Assault Vest automatically disables selecting status moves (tagged AV BLOCKED), adhering to tournament rules.
  • Switching Move Synchronization: Pivot moves like U-Turn and Volt Switch deal damage and immediately allow you to bring in a benched teammate on the same turn.

🔄 Championship Tactics & Pro Strategies

Double Pivoting & Board Positioning

In competitive Doubles (VGC), momentum is everything. Leading with a pairing like Incineroar + Rillaboom allows you to cycle Fake Out flinches, drop the opponent's offensive stats with Parting Shot, and cycle your heavy sweepers (like Archaludon or Ursaluna) into favorable positions without taking damage.

⚡ Dynamic Mid-Turn Speed Shifts

Following modern official competitive engine standards (Gen 7/8/9), turn order is dynamically recalculated mid-turn:

  • Mega Evolution Speed Shifts: As soon as a Pokémon Mega Evolves (such as Gengar Mega Evolving into Mega Gengar with 130 Base Speed), its new speed stat takes effect immediately on that exact turn. Slower actions queued for later in the turn are re-ordered behind it!
  • Tailwind Mid-Turn Acceleration: When a teammate or opponent executes Tailwind (+100% team Speed for 4 turns), the dynamic speed recalculation immediately pushes the remaining partners up the action queue on that very turn.

🛡️ Signature Defensive Abilities: Disguise & Multiscale

  • Mimikyu's Disguise: Protects Mimikyu from taking HP damage from the very first damaging attack it receives. When hit by a damaging move, its disguise busts and Mimikyu takes a minor recoil penalty of 1/8 (12.5%) max HP. Attacks that deal 0 damage due to type immunity (such as a Dragon-type Draco Meteor targeting Fairy/Ghost Mimikyu) do NOT bust Disguise! Attacks from Pokémon possessing Mold Breaker, Teravolt, or Turboblaze bypass Disguise completely and deal direct damage.
  • Dragonite's Multiscale: When Dragonite is at 100% full HP, all incoming damage from attacks is cut in half (50% reduction)! This allows Dragonite to easily survive normally fatal 4× Ice-type attacks to set up Dragon Dance or retaliate.
  • Kingambit's Supreme Overlord: Kingambit receives a +10% boost to attack power for every ally on its team that has fainted (up to +50% in standard 6v6 battles), turning it into a devastating late-game reverse sweeper.

👻 Semi-Invulnerable Moves & Protect Piercing

  • Phantom Force & Shadow Force: On Turn 1, the Pokémon vanishes into deep shadows in a semi-invulnerable state, avoiding incoming attacks. On Turn 2, the strike lands with full force. Crucially, Phantom Force and Shadow Force bypass and break opposing Protect, Detect, and Max Guard, leaving the opponent exposed for the rest of the turn!
  • Fly, Dig, Dive & Bounce: Enter elevated/underground semi-invulnerable states on Turn 1 and strike on Turn 2, evading ground-level or standard attacks during their charging turn.

🧸 Generation 9 Substitute Mechanics & Decoy Protection

Substitute is a cornerstone competitive tactical move that creates a decoy doll at the cost of 25% max HP:

  • Cost & Usage Limit: Costs exactly 25% of the user's max HP ($\lfloor\text{max\_hp} \times 0.25\rfloor$). The move strictly fails if the user's remaining HP is $\le 25\%$ max HP (preventing self-KO) or if a substitute is already active.
  • Zero Spillover Damage Rule: Incoming attacks strike the substitute first. Even if an attack inflicts 500 damage against a substitute with only 1 HP remaining, the substitute breaks and the Pokémon sustains 0 spillover damage!
  • Full Status & Secondary Effect Immunity:
    • Direct status moves (Will-O-Wisp, Toxic, Thunder Wave, Spore, Yawn, Taunt, Attract, Trick) fail against a target behind a substitute.
    • Secondary attack procs (flinching, secondary stat drops, burn/paralysis/freeze/poison, Knock Off item stripping) are completely blocked.
    • Defender held items and abilities are shielded: Focus Sash does not pop, Air Balloon does not burst, and Rough Skin / Rocky Helmet contact recoil does not trigger.
  • Bypass Exceptions (Gen 6+):
    • Sound-based moves (Boomburst, Bug Buzz, Hyper Voice, Torch Song, Snarl, Roar, Perish Song) bypass the substitute completely to damage the Pokémon directly.
    • Pokémon with the Infiltrator ability (e.g. Dragapult) bypass the substitute directly on all attacks.
  • Team Baton Passing & Shed Tail: An active substitute (and its remaining HP) is passed to incoming teammates via Baton Pass. Additionally, Shed Tail (Cyclizar, Orthworm) cuts 50% max HP, creates a 25% max HP substitute, and pivots out to gift the decoy to the switch-in.
  • HUD Visualization: The active Pokémon card renders a 🧸 SUB (X HP) badge while protected.

🛡️ Consecutive Protection Decay & Failure Formula

In official competitive Pokémon (Generations 6 through 9), defensive protection moves cannot be spammed indefinitely. Each consecutive use suffers a sharp exponential decay in accuracy:

  • The $(1/3)^n$ Degradation Formula:
    • 1st Turn: 100% success rate ($1/1$).
    • 2nd Consecutive Turn: 33.3% success rate ($1/3$).
    • 3rd Consecutive Turn: 11.1% success rate ($1/9$).
    • 4th Consecutive Turn: 3.7% success rate ($1/27$).
    • 5th Consecutive Turn: 1.2% success rate ($1/81$).
    • 6th Consecutive Turn: 0.4% success rate ($1/243$).
  • Shared Protection Moves: The consecutive counter is shared across all protection variants: Protect, Detect, Spiky Shield, Baneful Bunker, King's Shield, Obstruct, Silk Trap, Burning Bulwark, Max Guard, and Endure.
  • Counter Reset Triggers: The consecutive streak immediately resets back to 0 (restoring 100% accuracy on the next attempt) if:
    • A protection move fails (e.g. rolls failure or is blocked).
    • The Pokémon uses any other move (damaging attack or non-protection status move).
    • The Pokémon switches out or is forced out.
  • Protection Targeting Scope: Protect and Detect only shield against moves that target the protecting Pokémon (damaging attacks and targeted status debuffs like Taunt, Will-O-Wisp, and Screech). Protect NEVER blocks self-targeting status moves (Bulk Up, Swords Dance, Calm Mind, Dragon Dance, Recover, Substitute, Shell Smash) or field/entry hazard moves (Stealth Rock, Spikes, Tailwind, Rain Dance)!
  • AI Protection Anti-Spam: Champion AI dynamically factors in this degradation formula. If Protect was used on the preceding turn, the AI receives a severe score penalty ($\Delta -120.0$), ensuring it attacks or pivots rather than gambling on a 33% or 11% survival roll.

🔋 PP Tracking, Pressure Ability & Struggle Fallback

Every move in battle has a dedicated Power Points (PP) pool that decrements on every turn it is used:

  • Standard Deduction: Successfully attempting a move consumes 1 PP from the Pokémon's move pool.
  • Pressure Ability: When an active Pokémon has the Pressure ability (e.g. Corviknight, Dusclops, Zapdos, Mewtwo), any opponent move that targets it consumes 2 PP instead of 1. Pressure also announces its presence upon entering the battlefield.
  • Zero PP Move Lock: Moves with 0 PP are greyed out and cannot be clicked in the simulator HUD.
  • Struggle Execution: If a Pokémon has exhausted all PP across all 4 of its moves, a dedicated Struggle button appears. Struggle deals 50 Base Power typeless physical damage (hitting Ghost types for neutral $1.0\times$), but inflicts recoil equal to 25% of the user's maximum HP.

🔥 Signature Absorption Abilities & Switch-In Hazard Faint Rule

  • Thermal Exchange (Baxcalibur):
    • Burn Immunity: Baxcalibur is completely immune to burns from status moves (e.g. Will-O-Wisp, Flame Orb) and secondary move effects (e.g. Scald, Flamethrower).
    • Attack Boost: Whenever Baxcalibur is struck by any damaging Fire-type attack, it sustains normal damage and immediately raises its physical Attack by +1 stage.
  • Well-Baked Body (Dachsbun):
    • Fire Immunity: Completely negates all damage from Fire-type attacks ($0.0\times$ damage multiplier).
    • Defense Boost: When targeted or struck by a Fire-type attack, Dachsbun immediately raises its Defense sharply by +2 stages.
  • Elemental Absorption Suite: The simulator also enforces complete damage immunity and procs for:
    • Earth Eater (Orthworm): Restores 25% max HP when targeted by Ground moves.
    • Water Absorb / Dry Skin: Restores 25% max HP when hit by Water moves.
    • Volt Absorb: Restores 25% max HP when hit by Electric moves.
    • Lightning Rod / Storm Drain: Draws Electric/Water moves, grants 0 damage, and raises Special Attack (+1 SpA).
    • Motor Drive: Grants 0 damage and raises Speed (+1 Spe) when hit by Electric moves.
    • Sap Sipper: Grants 0 damage and raises Attack (+1 Atk) when hit by Grass moves.
    • Flash Fire: Grants 0 damage from Fire moves and activates a 1.5× boost to the user's own Fire attacks.
  • Hazard Faint Switch-In Ability Suppression:

    In accordance with official Pokémon championship rules, if a Pokémon faints upon switching in due to entry hazards (such as Stealth Rock, Spikes, or Steelsurge), its switch-in ability (Intimidate, Drizzle, Drought, Electric Surge, Psychic Surge, Pressure, etc.) DOES NOT ACTIVATE. The trainer is prompted immediately to select another Pokémon to send out into battle.

💥 High Jump Kick & Jump Kick Crash Recoil

High-power jumping attacks carry extreme competitive risk:

  • If High Jump Kick (130 BP) or Jump Kick (100 BP) misses its target due to an accuracy roll, or if the target blocks with Protect, Detect, or Max Guard, the attacker crashes to the ground and sustains 50% of their maximum HP as crash recoil damage!

⚡ Booster Energy & Paradox Protosynthesis / Quark Drive

  • Instant Turn-1 Consumption: When a Paradox Pokémon (e.g. Flutter Mane, Iron Bundle, Roaring Moon, Iron Hands) holds a Booster Energy, the item is consumed immediately on its first active turn on the battlefield.
  • Stat Amplification: Grants a +30% boost to the Pokémon's highest stat (+50% if the highest stat is Speed) without requiring manual Sun (Drought) or Electric Terrain setup.

⚡ Special Move Mechanics: Electro Shot, Freeze-Dry & Body Press

  • Last Respects & Kingambit Supreme Overlord (Fallen Ally Scaling):
    • Last Respects (Ghost Physical): Signature move of Houndstone and Basculegion. Starts at 50 Base Power and dynamically gains +20 BP for each fainted ally on the user's team ($\text{Power} = 50 + 20 \times \text{allies}$). With 5 teammates fainted, Last Respects unleashes a devastating 150 Base Power physical Ghost attack! Active BP is dynamically reported in the HUD move details and combat log.
    • Supreme Overlord (Kingambit Ability): Grants a +10% damage multiplier for every fainted ally on Kingambit's team, capping at +50% (1.5× total damage) when 5 allies have fallen. When Kingambit enters battle with fallen allies, Supreme Overlord triggers an entry announcement communicating its heightened power.
  • Multi-Hit Moves, Skill Link & Loaded Dice Suite:
    • 2–5 Hit Moves (Icicle Spear, Bullet Seed, Rock Blast, Scale Shot, Tail Slap, Pin Missile, Arm Thrust): Normally land 2 hits (35%), 3 hits (35%), 4 hits (15%), or 5 hits (15%).
    • Skill Link Ability (Cloyster, Cinccino): Guarantees the maximum 5 hits (100%) on every execution!
    • Loaded Dice Held Item (Baxcalibur, Breloom): Guarantees 4 or 5 hits (50%/50%), eliminating low 2-hit or 3-hit rolls. For Population Bomb, guarantees at least 4 to 10 hits!
    • Fixed Multi-Hit Moves: Dragon Darts (2 hits, 50 BP each), Dual Wingbeat (2 hits, 40 BP each), Surging Strikes (3 hits, 25 BP each, always crits), Triple Dive (3 hits, 30 BP each).
    • Substitutes & Disguise Bypass: In competitive Pokémon, multi-hit moves are the ultimate counter to decoys! Hit 1 busts Mimikyu's Disguise, while hits 2, 3, 4, and 5 deal full damage directly to Mimikyu's HP. Against a Substitute, strikes deplete the decoy; if broken, all subsequent strikes hit the Pokémon directly!
    • Focus Sash & Sturdy Shattering: Multi-hit moves easily bypass Focus Sash and Sturdy — the initial lethal strike reduces the target to 1 HP, and the subsequent strikes knock the defender out!
    • On-Hit Synergies: Each individual hit triggers Archaludon's Stamina (+1 Defense per hit, up to +5 in one turn) and increments defender's Rage Fist counter!
  • Poison-Type Toxic Accuracy Bypass:
    • Since Generation 6 and continuing in Gen 9, if Toxic is used by any Poison-type Pokémon (e.g. Clodsire, Toxapex, Gengar, Glimmora), it bypasses all accuracy checks to never miss!
    • Furthermore, Poison-type Toxic hits opponents even during semi-invulnerable turns (Fly, Bounce, Dig, Dive)! Non-Poison Pokémon retain standard 90% accuracy.
  • Generation 9 Status Conditions & Mechanics:
    • Burn: Deals 1/16 (6.25%) max HP chip damage at the end of each turn. Cuts the Pokémon's effective physical Attack stat by 50% (0.5×). Pokémon with the Guts ability completely ignore the attack penalty and gain a +50% (1.5×) Attack boost instead! Fire-types are immune to burn; Baxcalibur's Thermal Exchange provides complete burn immunity.
    • Regular Poison: Deals 1/8 (12.5%) max HP chip damage at the end of each turn. Poison and Steel types are completely immune.
    • Bad Poison (Toxic): Deals escalating damage equal to $N/16$ max HP ($1/16$ on Turn 1, $2/16$ on Turn 2, $3/16$ on Turn 3, etc.). The damage counter resets to 1 upon switching out.
    • Sleep: Lasts 1 to 3 turns, preventing moves (except Sleep Talk/Snore). Electric Terrain and Misty Terrain prevent grounded Pokémon from falling asleep. Waking up allows acting on that same turn.
    • Freeze: Preserved in Gen 9 SV (unlike Legends: Arceus Frostbite). Has a 20% chance to thaw out naturally each turn. Using thawing Fire moves (Flare Blitz, Flame Wheel, Sacred Fire, Scald, Pyro Ball) immediately thaws the user before attacking! Damaging Fire attacks also thaw frozen targets. Ice-types cannot be frozen.
    • Paralysis: Reduces Speed by 50% (0.5×) (in Gen 7+) and gives a 25% chance of being fully paralyzed on each turn. Electric-types and Limber Pokémon are immune.
  • Rage Fist Dynamic Hit Scaling (Annihilape / Primeape Signature Move):
    • Base Power Progression: Starts at 50 Base Power and increases by +50 BP for every damaging attack that hits the user, capping at an enormous 350 Base Power (after 6 hits).
    • Any Damaging Attack Counts: Unlike a common misconception, Rage Fist does not require contact moves — any physical or special attack that deals direct damage to the Pokémon increases the counter! Hits absorbed by Mimikyu's Disguise also increment the counter.
    • Multi-Hit Move Stacking: Each individual strike of a multi-hit move (such as Dragon Darts, Icicle Spear, Bullet Seed, or Population Bomb) counts as a separate hit, allowing Annihilape to gain multiple stacks in a single turn!
    • Permanent Battle Persistence: The hits counter does not reset upon switching out to the bench or fainting. Once Annihilape absorbs hits, Rage Fist retains its accumulated power for the remainder of the battle.
    • Exceptions: Attacks absorbed by a Substitute, blocked by Protect, passive hazard damage (Stealth Rock, Spikes), weather chip (Sandstorm), status damage (Burn, Poison), and self-confusion damage do not increment the counter.
    • Iron Fist Synergy: As a recognized punching move, Rage Fist gains an additional 20% (1.2×) damage boost from the Iron Fist ability.
  • Fixed-Damage Attacks & Suicide Moves (Final Gambit, Seismic Toss, Super Fang, Explosion): Specialized moves that deal direct, calculable damage independent of standard defensive stats and base power formulas:
    • Final Gambit (Fighting Special): Inflicts flat damage equal to the user's exact current HP at the moment of execution, and the user immediately faints. Bypasses defenses, screens, and Multiscale. However, if the opponent is a Ghost-type, the attack fails ($0\times$ immunity), deals 0 damage, and the user does not faint! Similarly, if blocked by Protect or if it misses, the user does not faint.
    • Seismic Toss & Night Shade: Deal fixed damage equal to the user's level (50 HP at level 50). Seismic Toss fails against Ghost-types; Night Shade fails against Normal-types.
    • Super Fang, Ruination & Nature's Madness: Deal flat damage equal to 50% of the defender's current HP. Super Fang is blocked by Ghost immunity; Ruination and Nature's Madness affect all types.
    • Endeavor: Sets the defender's current HP equal to the user's current HP (inflicting current HP difference). Fails against Ghost-types.
    • Explosion & Self-Destruct: High base power moves (250 BP / 200 BP) where the user self-destructs (faints) after attacking.
    • Tournament Tiebreak Rule: If both trainers lose their last Pokémon to a suicide move (Final Gambit or Explosion), official competitive rules dictate that the user of the suicide move loses the battle.
  • Self-Stat Drop Drawback Attacks (Clanging Scales, Close Combat, Draco Meteor): High-power attacks with self-stat penalties execute their drops immediately upon successfully landing damage (or hitting an opponent's Substitute):
    • Clanging Scales (110 BP Dragon Special): Sound-based signature attack of Kommo-o that bypasses Substitute and lowers the user's Defense by 1 stage (-1 Def).
    • Close Combat, Armor Cannon & Headlong Rush: Lowers Defense and Special Defense by 1 stage (-1 Def, -1 SpD).
    • Superpower: Lowers Attack and Defense by 1 stage (-1 Atk, -1 Def).
    • Draco Meteor, Overheat & Leaf Storm: Harshly lowers Special Attack by 2 stages (-2 SpA).
    • Make It Rain: Lowers Special Attack by 1 stage (-1 SpA).
    • Canonical Rule: If the attack misses, is blocked by Protect, or deals 0 damage due to type immunity (e.g. Clanging Scales against a Fairy-type Pokémon), no user stats are dropped.
  • Electro Shot in Rain vs Two-Turn Charging: Under normal conditions, Electro Shot is a two-turn move: on Turn 1 it charges and boosts Special Attack by +1 stage (+50%), and on Turn 2 it unleashes a 130 Base Power Electric blast. Under Rain (Drizzle or Rain Dance), Electro Shot executes in a single turn, granting +1 SpA and attacking immediately!
  • Freeze-Dry Super-Effective Override: Freeze-Dry is a specialized Ice-type attack (70 BP) that is super-effective (2×) against Water-type Pokémon instead of resisted! It deals a catastrophic 4× damage to dual-type Water/Dragon Pokémon (Kingdra, Dracovish, Walking Wake) and Water/Ground Pokémon (Swampert, Gastrodon, Quagsire).
  • Weather Ball Dynamics: Normally a 50 BP Normal-type move. In weather, its power doubles to 100 Base Power and changes type: Water in Rain, Fire in Sun, Ice in Snow, and Rock in Sandstorm.
  • Body Press Defense Substitution: Calculates damage using the user's current physical Defense stat (including Iron Defense boosts) instead of Attack. However, because it remains a Fighting-type attack, Ghost-type opponents retain 100% immunity (0× damage)!

🌧️ Weather & Terrain Wars

Controlling field effects can completely turn the tide of battle:

  • Sandstorm Rock-Type Special Defense Boost (1.5×): In a Sandstorm (Sand Stream or Sandstorm), all Rock-type Pokémon receive an automatic +50% (1.5×) boost to their Special Defense! For example, Tyranitar's effective Sp. Def increases from 100 to 150 base equivalent, significantly cushioning super-effective special hits like Moonblast or Focus Blast. Non-Rock/Ground/Steel Pokémon take 1/16 max HP chip damage each turn.
  • Snow Ice-Type Physical Defense Boost (1.5×): In Snow (Generation 9 canonical mechanic replacing Hail), all Ice-type Pokémon receive an automatic +50% (1.5×) boost to their physical Defense! This dramatically increases the physical bulk of defensive Ice types like Baxcalibur, Alolan Ninetales, and Cetitan without inflicting residual hail damage.
  • Rain Accuracy Bypasses (Thunder & Hurricane): Under Rain (Drizzle or Rain Dance), both Hurricane and Thunder bypass accuracy checks and cannot miss (100% accuracy), unless negated by weather-suppressing abilities like Cloud Nine or Air Lock, or replaced by sun/snow/sand! In Harsh Sun, their accuracy plummets to 50%.
  • Snow Accuracy Bypass (Blizzard): In Snow or Hail, Blizzard bypasses accuracy checks and cannot miss (100% accuracy).
  • Electric Terrain: Boosts Electric moves by 30% and prevents all grounded Pokémon from falling asleep, completely countering Amoonguss's Spore.
  • Psychic Terrain Priority Shield: Boosts Psychic moves by 30% and protects all grounded Pokémon from incoming priority attacks!

💥 Doubles Spread Damage Scaling (75% vs 100%)

In competitive Doubles (4v4), spread moves that strike both opponents simultaneously (such as Muddy Water, Bleakwind Storm, Heat Wave, Earthquake, Dazzling Gleam, and Rock Slide) undergo official VGC damage scaling:

  • Dual Targets Present: If both opposing Pokémon are active on the field, the attack deals 75% (0.75×) scaled damage to each target.
  • Solo Target Remaining: If one opposing Pokémon has already fainted or switched out, the spread penalty is removed, dealing 100% (1.0×) full damage to the lone defender!

🔮 Defensive Screens & Aurora Veil Mitigation

  • Light Screen: Reduces all incoming Special damage by 50% in Singles (33% in Doubles) for 5 turns (8 turns with Light Clay).
  • Reflect: Reduces all incoming Physical damage by 50% in Singles (33% in Doubles) for 5 turns (8 turns with Light Clay).
  • Aurora Veil: Can only be activated during Snow or Hail; establishes both Reflect and Light Screen simultaneously in a single turn!
  • Active screens and remaining turn countdowns are clearly visible in the simulator arena.

🎒 Item Swapping, Choice Move Locks & Mega Stones

  • Trick & Switcheroo: Swaps held items between the user and the target. This can be used offensively to cripple an opposing defensive wall (like Amoonguss or Ting-Lu) by giving them a Choice item.
  • Mega Stone Anti-Removal Security: Mega Stones (58 canonical stones) are permanently attached competitive artifacts. They cannot be removed by Knock Off, nor swapped using Trick or Switcheroo!
  • Choice Items (Band, Specs, Scarf):
    • Choice Band: Boosts Attack by +50% (1.5×).
    • Choice Specs: Boosts Special Attack by +50% (1.5×).
    • Choice Scarf: Boosts Speed by +50% (1.5×).
    • Choice Lock Drawback: The user is locked into repeating the very first move they select. They cannot choose any other move until they switch out! If a Pokémon holding a Choice item uses Trick, they swap items, release their Choice lock, and can select any move freely on the following turn. Conversely, the recipient immediately becomes Choice-locked on their next move.
  • Assault Vest: Boosts Special Defense by +50% (1.5×), but completely prevents the bearer from selecting any status moves.
  • Air Balloon: Grants the holder complete immunity to Ground-type attacks and entry hazards (Spikes, Toxic Spikes) until popped by a direct damaging hit.
  • Weakness Policy: When the holder is struck by a super-effective attack ($> 1.0\times$ effectiveness) and survives with remaining HP, Weakness Policy is consumed and sharply boosts both Attack (+2) and Special Attack (+2) stages (+100% damage)! Ideal on bulky dynamax/terastallized tanks like Tyranitar, Dragonite, and Metagross. Does not trigger if the holder faints or if hitting a Substitute.
  • White Herb: Single-use cleansing herb automatically consumed whenever any stat stage drops below neutral ($< 0$). It resets all lowered stats back to 0 while preserving positive stat boosts! Triggers on Intimidate, Sticky Web, self-stat drop attacks (Close Combat, Draco Meteor, Clanging Scales), status self-drops (Shell Smash clearing -1 Def/-1 SpD while preserving +2 Atk/+2 SpA/+2 Spe), and opponent stat debuffs.
  • Power Herb: Single-use herb allowing two-turn charging attacks to strike immediately on turn one without charging! Works with Meteor Beam (+1 SpA and immediate attack), Solar Beam, Solar Blade, Skull Bash (+1 Def and immediate attack), Geomancy (instant +2 SpA, +2 SpD, +2 Spe), Sky Attack, and two-turn semi-invulnerable attacks (Fly, Bounce, Phantom Force).

⚡ Priority Brackets & Sucker Punch Rules

  • Fake Out: Has +3 Priority, but only works on the very first turn the user enters the field (turnsOnField == 1). If used on subsequent turns without switching out first, it automatically fails!
  • Sucker Punch: Has +1 Priority, but only succeeds if the target selects a damaging attack. If the opponent uses a status move (like Swords Dance or Protect) or switches out, Sucker Punch automatically fails!
  • Psychic Terrain Priority Blocking: Grounded Pokémon targeted by priority moves (Sucker Punch, Fake Out, Extreme Speed, Aqua Jet) are protected by the terrain and take 0 damage.
  • Prankster: Grants +1 priority to status moves (e.g., Tailwind, Spore, Taunt), but Dark-type opponents are completely immune to Prankster-boosted moves.

🔥 Status Conditions & Item Management

Managing non-volatile status effects is crucial for championship survival:

  • Burn: Cuts physical attack damage by 50% and chips 1/16 HP per turn. However, Pokémon with the Guts ability (like Ursaluna) gain a 50% Attack boost instead!
  • Lum Berry: Automatically consumed to cure any non-volatile status condition (burn, freeze, paralysis, sleep, poison) or confusion immediately upon infliction.
  • Flame Orb: Activates at the end of Turn 1 to trigger Guts without taking outside sleep or paralysis.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions & Troubleshooting

How do I reset or back up my data?

Go to the 🏠 Home page and scroll down to the ⚡ Cache Diagnostics section. You can backup, clear, or recover caches for Pokémon, Moves, Abilities, Items, and Teams with one click.

Why can't I Mega Evolve my Pokémon?

Mega Evolution strictly requires holding the specific Mega Stone as your held item (e.g. Charizardite X/Y for Charizard, Lucarionite for Lucario, Mewtwonite X/Y for Mewtwo, Rayquaza knowing Dragon Ascent, and canonical forms like Mega Z). Check your held item in the Teambuilder or Slot Editor.

Why is Heatran marked not legal in Champions format?

Champions format legality strictly follows the official Serebii Champions roster (data/champions_roster.json). Heatran is currently banned from Champions tournament play. Conversely, competitive staples like Archaludon are fully legal.

How do I cleanly purge old AI battle knowledge?

Click the Reset Knowledge button in the Simulator or submit POST /api/simulator/knowledge/reset. This flushes both active in-memory caches and disk cache simultaneously, preventing stale or phantom knowledge from reappearing.

Why did my move fail?

Check the battle log! The move might have failed due to type immunities (e.g. Body Press on Ghost), Sucker Punch conditionality, terrain protections (Psychic Terrain blocking priority), or Protect shields.
